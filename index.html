Back · the three reintroductions and the stop rules

Where this stands. MRI of 8 Nov is clinically negative: no marrow oedema on STIR, no fracture, no nerve compression, nothing dangerous. Facet fluid at L3-L4 and L4-L5, small disc bulge at L4-L5, dehydrated discs, old Schmorl’s nodes. Every one of those appears in a large share of pain-free 37-year-olds. The scan did not explain the pain. It gave permission to load.

You got to 0 to 1 out of 10 by removing three things. That is avoidance, not tolerance. A back that is quiet only while flat speed, lumbar gym and descents are all absent has not recovered, it has been hidden. The point of the next seven weeks is to find out which of the three is actually a problem.

Why uphill is free and flat speed is not

Everything that provokes you combines axial load with lumbar rotation or extension: fast downhill, flat intervals, standing overhead press. Uphill running minimises all three, which is why Z4 climbing has been painless while slow flat running still feels slightly off. The cleanest signal in the whole picture: standing extension hurts and unloaded prone press-up does not. Same movement, and the difference is bodyweight on top of it. That is a load-dependent structure, not a range-dependent one.

Key sessions · how to run them
Strides

6–8 × 20 s at ~3:15–3:30/km, flat. Full 60–90 s walk/jog recovery. Relaxed, tall, fast turnover. Coordination drill, never breathless. 2–3×/week.

Short hill sprints

The descent ladder · the governing progression of this block

Descent is the original provocateur and the single most important adaptation for July 2027, so it gets rebuilt deliberately rather than deferred to January. 500 m (Sat 21 Nov) → 450 m (Wed 25 Nov) → 550 m (Wed 2 Dec) → 700 m (Wed 16 Dec) → 700 m plus one session on tired legs (Sat 19 Dec). Speed is held constant at roughly 85% of comfortable downhill until W13. The number that progresses is metres, not pace.

The original plan put 900 m of descent in one session on 24 Nov. Both independent reviews named it the most dangerous item in the whole block, arriving nine days after descent restarts from zero. It becomes 450 m. The 900 m session has a place and it is February 2027.

Shoes for descent work: Cloudultra Pro or Mafate X, not the Superblast 2. A soft single-slab high-stack shoe on sustained descent raises trunk stabilisation demand, which is the opposite of what this back wants. Retire the Speedgoat 5 at ~700 km rather than using it here.

Hagaparken hill repeats (Stockholm)

Frösundatoppen is 72 m with 62 m of prominence, steep enough that most runners walk the steepest part. 15 min easy to the park, then 10 to 14 repeats: up at HR 158 to 164, then down under control, which is the half that matters. Cues: high cadence, short steps, quiet feet, relaxed shoulders, eyes 4–5 m ahead not at your feet, arms wide, land under your centre of mass, lean with the slope not back, don't brake with an extended leg. Hagaparken at 12 repeats gives about 780 m of descent every week; Sigulda gave 450 to 1,050 m per week averaged. Weekly eccentric volume goes up. What you lose is sustained descent duration, 60 to 90 s per rep against 2 to 3 min at Sigulda. Fine for a base block, but long-descent work needs to come back in the Feb to May block before July.

Why the legs get the time

Strength training has not been shown to prevent injury in runners specifically; the well-known one-third reduction comes mostly from team sports and military recruits. The reasons to lift heavy are tendons, which only remodel above roughly 70% of maximum load, bone, power, which declines earlier and faster than strength with age, and running economy. All four point at the legs.

Heavy calves, why and how. At 64 kg, bodyweight calf raises train endurance, not the Achilles. Tendons need loads above about 70% of maximum, held for 12 weeks or more, and calf and Achilles become the most likely injury sites through your forties. If you get sick · the re-entry ladder
Neck check first. Fever, muscle aches, or any below-the-neck symptom = absolute rest. Myocarditis after a viral illness is rare but not theoretical.
The ladder
Day 0 → symptom-free +24 h: zero training. Not "easy", zero. Walking and the foot routine only. Eat at full training intake minus ~300 kcal. Don't drop into a deficit while ill; that's how the SHBG flag worsens and the second illness follows the first.
Days 1–3 post symptom-free: 30–45 min Z1 (≤130), flat, alternate days if RHR is still >3 bpm over baseline. Gate to advance: RHR within 3 bpm of baseline, two consecutive mornings.
Days 4–7: daily easy running at 60–70% of pre-illness weekly duration, nothing over 90 min. No strides, no hills, no loaded gym.
Days 8–14: one long run at 60% of pre-illness longest; reintroduce strides. First quality no sooner than 14 days post symptom-free, and it's sub-threshold, never threshold.
Ramp cap: don't exceed the last completed pre-illness week's hours until two consecutive weeks at ≥85% of it are banked.
Plan-level rules · decided now, while healthy
10 days or more lost: the block downgrades, it does not compress. Re-enter on the ladder. The reintroduction order resumes where it stopped, one new variable per week. Do not reclaim missed long runs; that is what turns a ten-day cold into a lost month.
The long-run spike rule applies on return. Your 30-day longest drops while you are ill, so the first long runs back are capped by it.

The 20-year view · what keeps a runner running

From two independent reviews on 16 Nov 2026, one in sports medicine and one in physiotherapy with strength and conditioning. Both named the same limit: no study has followed runners for 20 years, and strength training has not yet been shown to prevent injury in runners specifically. Read this as the best-reasoned bet, not proven fact.

Four levers that matter more than any exercise
1. Long-run spikes. No single run more than 10% longer than your longest in the previous 30 days. The strongest runner-specific evidence on this page.
2. Energy and bone. SHBG still unexplained, no DEXA yet. If under-fuelling turns out to be the cause, this moves to the top of the list.
3. Sleep quality. You are in bed long enough; the gap is efficiency.
4. Illness. Flu vaccine, hand hygiene on flights, and a gradual return after every illness.

Other sports
Cross-country skiing: the best winter option. Low risk, can replace an easy run.
Bouldering: fine. Down-climb rather than jump off, and pause 2 to 3 weeks before an A-race.
Swimming: fine for aerobic recovery; does nothing for bone.
Football or futsal: the most bone and agility value and the most acute risk; former Finnish elite footballers had twice the knee arthritis of runners. After July 2027, once a week, small-sided, with the FIFA 11+ warm-up every time, which cuts injuries by about 39%.
Alpine skiing: a holiday sport. Not in the last 6 to 8 weeks before a goal race.

Gym jumps give most of the bone benefit of a variable sport at a fraction of the acute risk. Sport longevity rankings, such as tennis adding nearly ten years, come from cohorts that contradict each other; do not choose a sport from them.

Yoga, Pilates, stretching
None of them prevents running injury. For low back pain, yoga and Pilates work about as well as other exercise, which you already do. Optional, and the first thing to cut when time is short. The upper-back routine is the exception, because it has a specific job.

Is 160 km in autumn 2027 realistic?

Marginally. It needs ~4,000 to 4,500 km in 2027 against ~2,900 in 2026, peak weeks of 14 to 16 h, two efforts past eight hours with a real night section, and an answer to what you eat at hour twenty. The gate: if the July 100k does not go well, Kullamannen moves to 2028. Decide that now, while it costs nothing.

The harder version of that advice: do not design 2027 around the 160. Designing for it is exactly what produces the +27% peak weeks that end in illness. Run the 100k in July, then decide in August on the evidence of how it went.
