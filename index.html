Back · the three reintroductions and the stop rules

Where this stands. MRI of 8 Nov is clinically negative: no marrow oedema on STIR, no fracture, no nerve compression, nothing dangerous. Facet fluid at L3-L4 and L4-L5, small disc bulge at L4-L5, dehydrated discs, old Schmorl’s nodes. Every one of those appears in a large share of pain-free 37-year-olds. The scan did not explain the pain. It gave permission to load.

You got to 0 to 1 out of 10 by removing three things. That is avoidance, not tolerance. A back that is quiet only while flat speed, lumbar gym and descents are all absent has not recovered, it has been hidden. The point of the next seven weeks is to find out which of the three is actually a problem.
The reintroduction order, and the rule that governs it
1 · Trap-bar deadliftTue 10 Nov
2 · DescentsSat 21 Nov
3 · Flat fast runningThu 3 Dec
Minimum 10 days between any two. Never two new stressors inside one 7-day window. Advance only after two consecutive clean exposures, meaning no session pain above 2 and no next-morning rise. One failure: repeat the same dose. Two consecutive failures: drop back a step and hold 10 days.
Trap-bar first, and it is the treatment rather than a stressor. Lumbar load tolerance is built by loading. Twelve weeks of avoiding the lift did not raise the ceiling.
Descents go on fresh legs until tolerance is proven. When the quads and glutes fatigue, impact absorption shifts up into the trunk. That is why the descent sessions sit on Saturday or mid-week rather than stacked behind the long run, until W13.
Why uphill is free and flat speed is not

Everything that provokes you combines axial load with lumbar rotation or extension: fast downhill, flat intervals, standing overhead press. Uphill running minimises all three, which is why Z4 climbing has been painless while slow flat running still feels slightly off. The cleanest signal in the whole picture: standing extension hurts and unloaded prone press-up does not. Same movement, and the difference is bodyweight on top of it. That is a load-dependent structure, not a range-dependent one.

Key sessions — how to run them
Strides

6–8 × 20 s at ~3:15–3:30/km, flat. Full 60–90 s walk/jog recovery. Relaxed, tall, fast turnover. Coordination drill — never breathless. 2–3×/week.

Short hill sprints

6–10 × 10–12 s near-maximal on 8–12%. Full 2–3 min walk down. Zero lactic intent. If rep 8 is slower than rep 2, you did too many. Ignore HR. 1–2×/week, all block.

Sub-threshold (flat or uphill)

HR 158–166, RPE 7. 2–3 min easy float jog between. Uphill version on 6–12% grade or treadmill at 8–12% — ignore pace entirely. The single most transferable session for the gravine climbs, and the answer to flat Riga.

The descent ladder — the governing progression of this block

Descent is the original provocateur and the single most important adaptation for July 2027, so it gets rebuilt deliberately rather than deferred to January. 500 m (Sat 21 Nov) → 450 m (Wed 25 Nov) → 550 m (Wed 2 Dec) → 700 m (Wed 16 Dec) → 700 m plus one session on tired legs (Sat 19 Dec). Speed is held constant at roughly 85% of comfortable downhill until W13. The number that progresses is metres, not pace.

The original plan put 900 m of descent in one session on 24 Nov. Both independent reviews named it the most dangerous item in the whole block, arriving nine days after descent restarts from zero. It becomes 450 m. The 900 m session has a place and it is February 2027.

Shoes for descent work: Cloudultra Pro or Mafate X, not the Superblast 2. A soft single-slab high-stack shoe on sustained descent raises trunk stabilisation demand, which is the opposite of what this back wants. Retire the Speedgoat 5 at ~700 km rather than using it here.

Hagaparken hill repeats (Stockholm)

Frösundatoppen is 72 m with 62 m of prominence, steep enough that most runners walk the steepest part. 15 min easy to the park, then 10 to 14 repeats: up at HR 158 to 164, then down under control, which is the half that matters. Cues: high cadence, short steps, quiet feet, relaxed shoulders, eyes 4–5 m ahead not at your feet, arms wide, land under your centre of mass, lean with the slope not back, don't brake with an extended leg. Hagaparken at 12 repeats gives about 780 m of descent every week; Sigulda gave 450 to 1,050 m per week averaged. Weekly eccentric volume goes up. What you lose is sustained descent duration, 60 to 90 s per rep against 2 to 3 min at Sigulda. Fine for a base block, but long-descent work needs to come back in the Feb to May block before July.

Strength · Upper A/B and Lower

Two Upper and two Lower-or-rehab sessions a week. The 3:1 upper-to-lower split was the wrong ratio for a runner with a left-sided back problem. The time moves to hinging, lateral chain and hip rotation.

Out until January: standing barbell overhead press, barbell bent-over row, back squat. The principle is narrow and it is the clearest signal in your whole presentation: loaded standing extension provokes you, loaded hinging does not. Standing overhead press is the worst possible match to that, and it was in your programme three times a fortnight while you were carefully avoiding deadlifts. The bent-over row is a sustained loaded hinge isometric with a rotation asymmetry on every rep. The back squat is simply redundant while the trap-bar is doing the same job with less extension demand.
The sessions are not the intervention, the eating is. Building muscle while running 100 km a week with SHBG at 80 and 83 resolves exactly one way: you eat more. The plan already wants +1 to 2 kg, and upper body mass is the best way to add it because it loads nothing you run on. Add the sessions without raising intake and you make the energy-availability flag worse, build nothing, and raise the illness risk that has already cost you two blocks this year.

Expect roughly 1 to 2 kg of genuine lean upper mass across fourteen weeks if the eating is right. That is a normal rate for a lean endurance athlete, not a disappointment.

Finding your starting loads
Week 1 is calibration, not training. For each lift pick a load you could do about 12 clean reps with, then do your prescribed 6 to 8. It will feel easy. That is the point: you learn the groove without a week of soreness that wrecks your running.
Week 2 onward: add load until the last set of 6 to 8 finishes with about two reps left. Then pure double progression, +2.5 kg when every set hits the top cleanly.

Rough entry points for a 63 kg runner with limited pressing history, just so you are not staring at the rack: bench 40 to 50 kg, overhead press 30 to 35 kg, barbell row 40 to 45 kg. Trust the rep test over the number.

Pull-ups and dips start at bodyweight. At 63 kg you will likely manage 8 to 12 pull-ups already. Add weight once you clear 10 clean.

Start dips at 2 sets, not 3, for the first fortnight. Dips load the chest and shoulder in a stretched position and are the most reliable source of first-time soreness here. Deep-breathing discomfort on a hard run three days later is avoidable. Build to 3 sets from week 3.

Expect soreness for two weeks, then little after. Upper body DOMS does not meaningfully affect running, with that one exception. Anything sharp rather than sore in the shoulder: stop that movement and tell me.

If you get sick — the re-entry ladder
Neck check first. Fever, muscle aches, or any below-the-neck symptom = absolute rest. You're 39; myocarditis after viral illness is not theoretical.
The ladder
Day 0 → symptom-free +24 h: zero training. Not "easy" — zero. Walking and the foot routine only. Eat at full training intake minus ~300 kcal. Don't drop into a deficit while ill — that's how the SHBG flag worsens and the second illness follows the first.
Days 1–3 post symptom-free: 30–45 min Z1 (≤130), flat, alternate days if RHR is still >3 bpm over baseline. Gate to advance: RHR within 3 bpm of baseline and HRV within 1 SD, two consecutive mornings.
Days 4–7: daily easy running at 60–70% of pre-illness weekly duration, nothing over 90 min. No strides, no hills, no loaded gym.
Days 8–14: one long run at 60% of pre-illness longest; reintroduce strides. First quality no sooner than 14 days post symptom-free, and it's sub-threshold, never threshold.
Ramp cap: don't exceed the last completed pre-illness week's hours until two consecutive weeks at ≥85% of it are banked.
